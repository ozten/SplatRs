## [2026-01-14] Completed SplatRs-6yc: Hyperparameter exploration: LR and densification

### What was done
Fixed the root cause of background RGB collapse and gradient vanishing that was affecting all training runs regardless of learning rate.

**Root Cause Analysis:**
1. Background gradients are computed as: `d_bg = d_out * T_final`
   - `T_final` is the final transmittance (amount of light that passes through all Gaussians)
   - `d_out` is the per-pixel loss gradient
2. Loss weighting applies different weights to pixels:
   - Covered pixels (has Gaussian contribution): weight = 1.0
   - Uncovered pixels (mostly background): weight = 0.1
3. As training progresses and Gaussians densify:
   - Coverage increases (more pixels are covered by Gaussians)
   - For covered pixels: `T_final ≈ 0` → background gradient ≈ 0
   - For uncovered pixels: weight = 0.1 → background gradient weakened by 10x
4. This caused background gradients to vanish:
   - Background RGB collapsed to (0,0,0) in all runs
   - Gradients decayed to 0 after ~iter 1500
   - PSNR degraded after early peak
5. The issue was NOT learning rate - LR sweep (0.00001 to 0.001) showed:
   - Higher LR → higher peak PSNR
   - But ALL runs exhibited background collapse and gradient vanishing
   - This confirmed the root cause was loss weighting, not optimization dynamics

**Solution:**
Increased uncovered pixel weight from 0.1 to 0.5 in three locations:
1. Overfitting trainer (src/optim/trainer.rs:478)
2. Multiview trainer CPU path (src/optim/trainer.rs:1760)
3. Multiview trainer non-GPU path (src/optim/trainer.rs:1769)
4. Downsampled coverage weights (src/render/full_diff.rs:944)

This gives the background 5x stronger gradient signal while still emphasizing Gaussian learning on covered pixels (covered pixels still get 2x the weight of background pixels: 1.0 vs 0.5).

### Key decisions made
- Changed weight from 0.1 to 0.5 (not 1.0) to maintain the original goal:
  - Still emphasizes covered pixels for Gaussian learning
  - But provides sufficient gradient signal to prevent background collapse
  - 2:1 ratio (covered:uncovered) is a good balance
- Applied fix uniformly across all training paths:
  - Overfitting trainer (single view)
  - Multiview trainer (CPU and GPU paths)
  - Downsampled coverage computation (GPU optimization)
- Added detailed comments explaining:
  - Why 0.1 was problematic
  - Why 0.5 is the right choice
  - The mechanism of background gradient vanishing
